The Scenario

The spotlight turns to you

The tea is poured. You've said khub shundor hoyeche and everyone smiled. Then Ammu sets down her cup, looks at you warmly, and asks: "Kaj kemon cholche?" — how's work going?

Every visit reaches this moment. They want to know about you — your work, your health, your family back home. Until now you could only smile and look at your wife. This lesson gives you real answers.
Core Phrases · Talking About Yourself

Six answers that carry the conversation

Short, honest, warm. You don't need long sentences — you need the right short ones.

Bhalo cholche
BHAA-lo CHOL-che
"It's going well" — your answer to "kaj kemon cholche?"
Literally "it's moving well". Works for work, projects, life in general. Your single most useful answer this lesson.
Ami khub byasto
AA-mi khub BYAS-to
"I'm very busy"
The honest follow-up when work is hectic. Often paired: "Bhalo cholche — khub byasto!" (Going well — very busy!). Universally understood and always sympathized with.
Shobai bhalo ache
SHO-bai BHAA-lo AA-che
"Everyone is well" — when they ask about your family
Shobai = everyone. When they ask about your parents or your side of the family, this is the warm, complete answer.
Alhamdulillah
al-HAM-du-lil-laah
"Praise be to God — all is well"
In a Muslim household, this is the most natural possible answer to "how are you / how's everything?" It expresses gratitude and wellbeing in one word. Using it will land beautifully.
You'll hear this constantly — as an answer, as a response to good news, after a meal. It works alone or after a phrase: "Bhalo achi, alhamdulillah."
Ektu ektu shikhchi
EK-tu EK-tu SHIKH-chi
"I'm learning little by little" — about your Bangla
The moment they realise you're speaking Bangla, someone will ask if you're learning. This is your answer — modest, charming, and it will delight the entire room. Possibly the highest-value phrase in this course.
Ar apni?
aar AP-ni
"And you?" — return the question
The conversation multiplier. Answer, then bounce it back: "Bhalo achi — ar apni?" Suddenly you're not being interviewed, you're having a conversation.
With friends (tumi) Ar tumi? — aar TU-mi — with your wife's friends, swap apni for tumi. "Bhalo achi — ar tumi?"
Listening Practice · What You'll Hear

The questions coming your way

These are the four questions you will actually be asked, in roughly this order. Recognise them, and you'll never be caught off guard.

Them
Kaj kemon cholche?
kaaj KE-mon CHOL-che · "How's work going?"
You
Bhalo cholche, dhônnobad. Ar apni?
"Going well, thank you. And you?"
If it's been hectic, add: "khub byasto!" — they'll nod knowingly.
Them
Apni ki kaj koren?
AP-ni ki kaaj KO-ren · "What work do you do?"
You
Ami [your job]-e kaj kori
AA-mi ...-e kaaj KO-ri · "I work in/at [your job]" — e.g. "Ami office-e kaj kori"
Your job title in English inside a Bangla sentence is completely normal — everyone does it. "Ami software-e kaj kori" works perfectly.
With friends (tumi) Tumi ki kaj koro? — TU-mi ki kaaj KO-ro — a friend asking you the same question. The -en ending (apni) becomes -o (tumi).
Them
Bangla shikhchen?!
BAANG-laa SHIKH-chen · "You're learning Bangla?!" — said with delight
You
Jee, ektu ektu shikhchi!
"Yes, I'm learning little by little!"
Expect applause, laughter, and someone immediately teaching you five more words. This is the best moment of the visit.
With friends (tumi) Bangla shikhcho? — BAANG-laa SHIKH-cho — how a friend would ask it. The -chen ending (apni) becomes -cho (tumi).
Them
Shoreer kemon?
SHO-reer KE-mon · "How's your health?"
You
Jee, bhalo. Alhamdulillah.
"Good, thank God."
You learned to ask this in Lesson 2 — now you can answer it too. Return it to an elder and watch them warm up: "Apnar shoreer kemon?"

Real-World Challenge
Before the next session

On the next call or visit, when anyone asks about your work or your day, answer in Bangla: "Bhalo cholche" — then return the question: "Ar apni?" (or "Ar tumi?" to a friend).

Gold-star version: find a moment to say "Ektu ektu Bangla shikhchi" to someone who doesn't know you're learning. Watch what happens.

And remember the family forms: it's Ammu, and it's pani.
