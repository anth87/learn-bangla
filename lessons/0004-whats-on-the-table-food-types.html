The Scenario

Dinner is served

The table fills up fast. Rice, then a bowl of dal, then fish, then something orange you don't recognise, then chicken, then a second rice that smells of cardamom. Ammu names each one as it lands: "Eta ilish mach. Eta murgi. Eta polao."

Right now those are just sounds. By the end of this lesson they're dishes you can name, ask about, and pick favourites from. Food is the topic every Bangladeshi family can talk about for hours. It's your easiest way into the conversation.

Rice layered with meat. Weddings, Eid, celebrations.

The comfort food. Rainy day = khichuri day. Ask about it and you'll get a story.

Weekend breakfast, usually with egg or alu.

Fish, meat & egg
The dish that gets the most attention
review
Mach
maach
Fish
"Mache-bhaate Bangali": rice and fish make a Bengali. Fish is identity, not just food.
Ilish mach
I-lish maach
Hilsa fish
The national fish. Bony, rich, prized. Praising the ilish is praising the cook.
Murgi
MUR-gi
Chicken
The everyday meat. "Murgir mangsho" in full, but "murgi" alone is normal at the table.
review
Mangsho
MANG-shô
Meat
The general word. Add the animal in front to be specific.
Gorur mangsho
GO-rur MANG-shô
Beef
Lit. "cow's meat". Central at Eid-ul-Adha.
Khashir mangsho
KHAA-shir MANG-shô
Goat (mutton)
The special-occasion meat. Often in the biriyani.
Dim
dim
Egg
Dim bhaji = fried egg / omelette. Breakfast staple.
Mangsho is the Shudho Bangla word and your primary target. In many Muslim households you'll also hear gosht (gosht), borrowed from Urdu. Both mean meat; if her family says gosht, follow them.

Using the Words · Three Frames

Drop any food word into these

Three tiny frames turn a list of nouns into conversation. The food word is the only part that changes.

Eta ki?
E-taa ki
"What is this?"
Point at a dish and ask. Her answer will be "Eta ___" (this is ___). Asking shows curiosity about her cooking, which is its own compliment.
Ami ___ khub pochondo kori
AA-mi ___ khub PO-chon-do KO-ri
"I really like ___"
"Ami ilish mach khub pochondo kori." Pick your five favourites from the plates above and practise them in this frame out loud.
Apni ki pochondo koren?
AP-ni ki PO-chon-do KO-ren
"What do you like?" (formal)
Turn it back to Ammu or Abba. Add two options with naki (NAA-ki, "or"): "Mach naki mangsho?"
With friends (tumi) Tumi ki pochondo koro? · TU-mi ki PO-chon-do KO-ro · same question to your wife's friends. The -en ending (koren) becomes -o (koro), exactly like kaj koren → kaj koro in Lesson 3.
Listening Practice · What You'll Hear

Around the table

Listen for the food word first. Even if the rest of the sentence is a blur, catching "ilish" or "jhal" tells you what's going on.

Ammu
Bhaat neen. Dal diye khan.
bhaat neen. daal DI-ye khaan · "Take rice. Eat it with dal."
You
Jee, dhônnobad.
"Yes, thank you."
Diye (DI-ye) = "with". You'll hear it constantly: dal diye bhaat, mach diye bhaat. Rice is the base; diye names what goes on it.
With friends (tumi) Bhaat nao. Khao, khao! · bhaat NAA-o. KHAA-o · a friend serving you. Neen → nao, khan → khao.
Ammu
Aaj ilish mach ranna korechi.
aaj I-lish maach RAAN-naa KO-re-chi · "Today I've cooked hilsa."
You
Ilish mach ami khub pochondo kori!
"I really love hilsa!"
Recognition only: ranna (RAAN-naa) = cooking. You don't need to say it yet, but when you hear it, someone is telling you what they made for you.
You
Eta ki?
"What's this?" (pointing at the orange mash)
Ammu
Eta alu bhorta.
E-taa AA-lu BHOR-taa · "This is mashed potato (spiced)."
You
Khub shundor hoyeche!
"It's come out beautifully!" Savoury dish, so shundor, not mishti.
Abba
Jhal lagche?
jhaal LAAG-che · "Is it too spicy for you?"
You
Ektu jhal. Pani deben?
"A little spicy. Could I have some water?" Or, if you're fine: "Na, thik ache."
Same shape as bhook lagche (I'm hungry): lagche = "is feeling". It has no apni/tumi ending, so a friend asks it exactly the same way.
Friend
Tumi ki pochondo koro, biriyani naki khichuri?
"What do you prefer, biriyani or khichuri?"
You
Biriyani! Ar tumi?
"Biriyani! And you?" Tumi back to a friend.

Real-World Challenge
Before the next session

At the next family meal (or on a call when food comes up), point at one dish and ask "Eta ki?" Listen for the food word in the answer.

Then tell Ammu one real favourite: "Ami ___ khub pochondo kori." Pick it now, so it's ready.

Gold-star version: ask her back, "Apni ki pochondo koren?" and see if you can catch the dish she names.
